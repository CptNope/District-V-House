## Users

- Primary: shoppers from Central Mass. and the Blackstone Valley (Worcester, Sutton, Millbury, Grafton, Northbridge…) who found District V House on Instagram or by driving past on Manchaug Road and need to know what it is, when it's open, and how to get there. One mapped review: "I drive by this store pretty frequently and never knew what it was" until Instagram.
- Secondary: residential and commercial clients looking for an interior designer (District V Design).
- Secondary: makers/vendors who want a space (currently at max capacity; applications via Instagram bio).
- Pitch audience: Jamie Harrity Burke, owner and designer, who has to see her store in it within one viewport.

## Product Purpose

District V House is a vendor-based home lifestyle store in the original village store of Manchaug, a historic 1800s mill village in Sutton, MA. It sells a changing mix of antique, vintage, old, new, handmade and artisan home decor, accessories and furniture, restyled every season. District V Design is Jamie's interior design practice (residential + commercial), rooted in Worcester. Success = people show up on open days (especially in holiday season), design clients reach Jamie, and the site stops being broken.

## Positioning

- Owned by a self-taught Worcester designer born and raised in Worcester's District 5, which is where the "V" comes from. She brings city design energy to a tiny mill village.
- A women-led vendor collective: all but one vendor are women. Jamie's motto: "Alone we are but one drop. Together we are an ocean."
- The store is never the same twice: open-plan, restyled with every season, maximalist holiday displays ("decked out to the MAX").
- A designer runs the shop: the floor is styled like rooms by someone who designs rooms for a living.

## Brand Commitments

- Names: "District V House" (store) and "District V Design" (studio). The Roman numeral V = Worcester's District 5.
- Existing mark: circular black-and-white badge with a small "DISTRICT V" over a large serif "HOUSE".
- Must not feel: cold/minimal, generic farmhouse, or cut off from its Worcester city roots.

## Evidence on Hand

Real: address, hours, phone, email, social handles and counts, event history, vendor policy text, owner story (The Faces of Worcester), wall decal products. Absent and not to be fabricated: testimonials beyond the mapped quote, vendor list beyond the two named, prices other than the decals, 2026 Stroll date.

## Product Principles

1. Open hours and directions are never more than one glance away; the store is only open four days a week.
2. Show the store changing; seasons are the content calendar.
3. Credit the collective: vendors are part of the product, not a footnote.
4. A designer's eye, a city kid's nerve, a mill village's history, in that order.
