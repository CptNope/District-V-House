District V House

Brand book and design system for the website: a designer's vendor-collective home store in the historic Manchaug Company Store, Sutton, Massachusetts, and its sister studio District V Design.

View the sitetokens.json

The idea: the shop window

The site works like the storefront of an 1800s village store seen at dusk: a transom with sign-painted gilt lettering, lit display panes divided by thick sash bars, and a painted bulkhead under the window that holds the hours and the door. Jamie restyles the whole store every season, so the window is repainted every season too. It refuses cream-and-script farmhouse and cold white minimalism. Tagline: A little unexpected. A lot extraordinary.

District V House

A curated, multi-vendor home lifestyle store: antique, vintage, old, new, handmade and artisan home decor, furniture, art and gifts. A small, exclusive group of vendors, nearly all women-owned. Every room tells a different story, restyled every season.

The V

Worcester's District 5, where owner Jamie Harrity Burke was born and raised. A self-taught designer since she was four or five. Explain it once wherever the brand is introduced.

The place

The historic Manchaug Company Store, overlooking the Mumford River in the 1800s mill village of Manchaug, Sutton. A store again, its original use. One mile from Route 146, near 290 and 395.

Voice

Warm, direct, a little bit city. Talk like Jamie on the shop floor, not like a catalog.

Say

  • Come shop.
  • Never the same store twice.
  • Born in District 5.
  • One floor, a whole collective.
  • Shop. Sip. Stay merry.

Avoid

  • Farmhouse clichés: gather, blessed, live laugh love
  • Luxury-speak: elevated, bespoke curation
  • Invented vendors, prices, reviews or dates
  • Adjectives where a noun works (tins, lamps, wreaths)

Color: the season is the palette

Every color token has four themes. The site picks the season from the date in Eastern time (Holiday Nov–Dec, Winter Jan–Mar, Garden Apr–Jul, Harvest Aug–Oct) and visitors can restyle it. Strategy: committed color in whole regions, not accents.

Type

Gloock is the sign-painter's gilt and matches the serif HOUSE badge: names, headings, captions, quotes, status. Hanken Grotesk sets everything else. No italics, no eyebrow labels above headings, no gradient text.

Layout, spacing & shape

The page is one storefront: header sash, transom, three panes, bulkhead, then full-width bands, each divided by a mullion-wide sash bar. The first viewport always shows the name, the season's panes, today's hours and Get directions. On phones a Directions / Call dock appears whenever those buttons scroll away.

Components

Button

Three painted buttons: **gilt** (primary, `leaf` fill, `sash` text, `shadow-button`), **line** (secondary on glass, 1.5px `leaf` border, `frost` text) and **dark** (on the gold Visit band, `sash` fill, `leaf` text). Radius `radius-md`, 15×22px padding, `button` text style, an 18px line icon on the left. One gilt button per region. The verb names the action ("Get directions", "Start a design project"). Phone numbers are buttons too: a `tel:` link showing the number. The consumer provides the href and icon.

OpenSign

The hanging OPEN/CLOSED sign on two chains, top right of the header sash. It reads the real time in America/New_York against the store hours (Thu–Sat 10–5, Sun 11–4) and shows **Open · until 5 pm** on `ornament` or **Closed · Opens Thursday at 10 am** on `glass-2`. 2px `leaf` border, `radius-sign`, `shadow-sign`, `status` word in Gloock. It swings once on load (off under reduced motion), links to the Visit section, and is `aria-live="polite"`. Keep hours in one place in code and in the schema.org data.

PriceTag

A string tag, like the ones on a vendor's booth, for the categories and vendor names in the Collective section. `leaf` tinted 30% into `frost`, `sash` text, a hole punched at the left, a notched left edge. Tags in a row tilt a degree or two at irregular intervals, the way real tags hang. Use on `frost` ground only. Words, not sentences. Never use it as a button.

GiltLettering

Sign-painted gold leaf: Gloock in `leaf`, a 1px lighter keyline on top, and a `gilt-shade` drop shade in the season's `shade`. For the arched name in the transom (an SVG textPath on a shallow quadratic arc, a shade copy offset 3,4 underneath), headings on glass, pane captions and the Stroll title. Only on `glass`, `glass-2` or photo panes under the glass scrim. Never on frost or gold. Never gradient-filled.

SeasonSwatch

The "Restyle the window" control: four toggle buttons (Holiday, Winter, Garden, Harvest), each with a three-chip strip (glass, leaf, ornament of that season), the season name and its months. `aria-pressed` marks the current one. Clicking dims the window panes, swaps the season theme and photos, and brings the lights back up. Sits on the `ornament` band in `on-ornament` color. Two columns on phones.

Logo

The black-and-white badge: a small DISTRICT V over a large serif HOUSE between two rules. On dark grounds, always on a white disc. Ask Jamie for a vector (SVG) master for print and the WordPress build.

Do keep it black on white, with clear space at least the height of the HOUSE letters.

Don't recolor it gold, stretch it, add effects, or place it on glass or photos without the white disc.

Photography

The store's own photos, shot with the store lights on, warm, full of objects, one subject per frame. Each window pane gets a gilt caption painted low on the glass. All 46 photos from districtvhouse.com live in assets/photos/ (synced by the GitHub Action); the ones marked in red are used on the site. Avoid stock, empty white cut-outs and posed people.

Iconography & motion

Line icons on a 24px grid, 1.8px stroke, round joins, currentColor. No emoji and no third-party logos (spell out Instagram, Facebook). Motion: one authored moment. When the season changes, the window's lights go down, the floor is restyled and the lights come back up; the OPEN sign swings once on load. Both are off under reduced motion.
